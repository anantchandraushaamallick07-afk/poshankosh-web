Our Journey • The Story Behind PoshanKosh®

The Ledger, the Late-Night Calculator, and Why PoshanKosh® Exists

Most software starts with a pitch deck or a market gap. Ours started with a father who came home from school too tired to eat, yet stayed awake past eleven o’clock every night—not because he wanted to, but because twelve paper registers refused to wait until morning.

1. The Dining Table at Dusk

To most people outside the school gates, a government teacher’s day ends when the afternoon bell rings. Growing up, I knew better. My father was a government school teacher, and at our home, the second shift began right after he washed his hands and sat down at the dining table.

Instead of resting, or asking us how our day went, he would pull out a worn cloth bag heavy with ruled registers—twelve of them, each thicker than a textbook—alongside loose market slips, a bank passbook, and a calculator whose keys had faded from years of thumb-pressing. Dinner would cool on the corner of the table while he muttered numbers under his breath.

“Give me ten minutes,” he would tell my mother, or us, whenever we asked him to join the family. “I just need to close today’s MDM column before tomorrow’s inspection.” We all knew ten minutes meant two hours. By the time he shut the final cover, everyone at home was already fast asleep.

2. Chasing Ten Paise at Midnight

Unless you have sat beside someone doing PM-POSHAN paperwork, it is hard to appreciate how unforgiving it is. A teacher cannot simply teach and come home. Every single school day demands exact grain math: 100 grams of rice per primary child and 150 grams for upper primary, multiplied by actual attendance, and tallied against opening stock and fresh receipts.

Then come the cooking cost slabs—₹6.78 and ₹10.17—split across dry rations, vegetables, fuel, eggs or fruit, ragi laddoos, iron and folic acid tablets, and the daily tasting register. When the last week of the month arrives, the real ordeal begins: drafting four separate monthly vouchers, logging every cheque withdrawal, and sitting down to reconcile the handwritten cashbook against the bank statement, line by line.

I watched my father redo an entire month’s totals at 11:30 PM because his closing balance was off by ten paise. Ten paise—smaller than any coin in circulation—could ruin an entire night’s sleep and steal the only quiet hours he had left with his family.

“A man who spent his whole morning shaping other people’s children was losing his own evenings to column totals. He wasn’t tired from teaching; he was exhausted from carrying an entire office back home.”

3. A Quiet Promise Made Without Fanfare

There wasn’t a dramatic lightning-bolt moment. Just an ordinary night when I saw my father rub his eyes over an open cashbook, a cold cup of water beside his elbow, and thought: Why are we asking teachers to live like full-time accountants with ballpoint pens?

He didn’t need a fancy corporate dashboard. He didn’t have a desktop computer in his classroom, and half the rural schools around us barely got one bar of mobile signal. Whatever I built had to live on an ordinary Android phone, work completely offline in the middle of a village, and feel simple enough that even a teacher who dislikes technology could finish a month’s work in minutes instead of giving up entire weekends.

4. Built Around Real Registers, Not Guesswork

I didn’t design PoshanKosh® by reading brochures. I built it by sitting next to real registers, asking teachers where totals break, which column causes audit headaches, and what format the block office actually asks for. Step by step, those real-world rules went into code:

Mark daily attendance once, and both rice consumption and cooking costs calculate automatically. Tap the three-dot Quick Restock inside any register, enter KG–5 and 6–8 shares, and the total updates on its own. Record a cheque withdrawal, and the contra entry reflects in the cashbook without double typing. Match a bank statement one-to-one, generate all four monthly vouchers (Vr 1 to Vr 4) along with the two-page bilingual MPR PDF—and put the phone away before evening tea even gets cold.

5. Where Your Records Live—And Where They Never Go

Because I grew up watching how carefully teachers guard their school cashbooks, trust had to be built into the architecture, not just written on a policy page. That is why we made two non-negotiable choices from day one:

Zero Financial Server Storage

PoshanKosh® never uploads, reads, or stores even a single entry of your school’s financial data on our servers. Everything stays encrypted on your own phone and inside your school’s private Google Drive (drive.appdata).

An Individual Effort, Not a Govt App

PoshanKosh® is an independent utility built by an individual for teachers. It is not a government app and makes no claim to represent any ministry or department—it simply exists to lighten your daily load.

6. About ₹119—No Spin, Just Plain Honesty

You deserve a straight answer about why this app costs ₹119 instead of being stuffed with ads:

There is no venture capital firm behind PoshanKosh®, no government grant, and no desire to flash annoying pop-up ads in front of a teacher trying to finish a register. Keeping an app alive takes real money every month—cloud servers, deployment pipelines, domain renewals, Play Store compliance, and countless late-night hours writing fixes whenever rules or Android versions change.

The ₹119 charge is nothing more than what it takes to keep those servers running cleanly and leave a modest pocket-money margin for the person maintaining it. It costs less than a single evening’s snacks—and in return, it gives you back fifteen hours of your life every month, completely ad-free and private.

7. For Every Evening That Belongs at Home

Awards and download counts don’t mean much next to the messages teachers send when they finish an entire month’s vouchers and bank matching in ten minutes—and realize they still have the whole evening left to spend with their family.

PoshanKosh® was built to give my father’s evenings back to every teacher across India. Thank you for letting it sit on your desk.
